Notice Simulator

Pick a notice telling cardholders their account is moving to a new bank, then pick a customer segment. A panel of simulated cardholders written by Claude predicts how that segment reacts. Each segment is marked trusted or not, based on how closely its panel matched real answers to the Federal Reserve's 2025 household survey.

Which notice wins where

Share of card holders in each segment who would set up the new login within a week. The best notice for a segment is only a recommendation when that segment's panel is trusted; otherwise it needs real research first.

Why some segments are trusted

Before anyone reads a reaction, every simulated person also answers seven questions the Federal Reserve asked about 13,000 adults. A segment is trusted when its panel lands within 10 points of the real answer on at least 6 of 7 questions. 0 of 8 segments passed in this run.

  1. Has a bank account
  2. Has a credit card
  3. Carried a card balance
  4. Used Buy Now, Pay Later
  5. Paid an overdraft fee
  6. Had credit card fraud
  7. Used a check casher or money order

How it works

One call, twenty people

Each Claude call writes 20 distinct people from one segment, with a profile, their seven survey answers and their reaction to every notice. 16 calls give 40 people per segment.

Check before you trust

Panel shares are computed on the same base the report uses (balances among card holders, overdraft fees among banked adults) and compared with the published table.

Read the reactions

Only card holders react. For each notice: set up the login within a week, first suspect a scam, and whether they would keep, use less or close the card.

Recorded, then replayed

Live runs are recorded with a fingerprint of the prompt and model. This page and CI replay the recording, so a changed prompt can never reuse an old answer.

Limits

  • Trust is measured on survey questions, not on reactions to notices. A panel that matches card ownership is a reason to take its reactions seriously, not proof they are right. A real pilot would back-test reactions against a wave of notices that already went out.
  • The model may have read this Federal Reserve report in training, which would flatter the match. The check shows agreement, not independent proof.
  • With 40 people per segment, a share moves in steps of a few points and carries sampling noise of up to about 8 points, which is why the tolerance is 10 points.
  • Segments are income bands and age bands, one at a time, because those are the rows the report publishes.

Data

Real: survey answers from the Federal Reserve's Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025 report (SHED, tables 31, 32, 35, 40, 41). Simulated: every person and reaction, written by claude-opus-5-5 in one recorded run (320 people, $2.35, about 59.4 s per call). Invented: the three notices and the banks named in them.

Run it yourself

Python 3.10+. Replays the recording, no key needed. Set NOTICESIM_LIVE=1 and an Anthropic API key to run a new panel.

pip install -e .
noticesim run notices/*.md
